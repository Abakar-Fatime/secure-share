# Detector evaluation

Generated by `backend/scripts/evaluate_detector.py`. The script simulates an organisation of 40 people over 30 days, injects labelled attacks in the last 10 days, and replays every event through the same rules the API runs (`backend/app/detector.py`), including alert de-duplication.

Runs: 5 random seeds (1, 2, 3, 4, 5); about 21,820 events per run.

## Detection of attacks

| Attack | Rule | Detected | Median actions before alert |
|---|---|---|---|
| brute force | `brute_force` | 50/50 (100%) | 5 |
| password spray | `password_spray` | 30/30 (100%) | 5 |
| stolen credentials abroad | `impossible_travel` | 40/40 (100%) | 1 |
| data exfiltration | `download_burst` | 39/40 (98%) | 20 |
| night login compromise | `off_hours` | 12/30 (40%) | 1 |
| link password guessing | `link_password_guessing` | 30/30 (100%) | 5 |
| mass link creation | `mass_link_creation` | 25/25 (100%) | 10 |
| mass deletion | `mass_deletion` | 25/25 (100%) | 15 |
| slow brute force *(evasive)* | `brute_force` | 0/20 (0%) | n/a |
| slow exfiltration *(evasive)* | `download_burst` | 2/20 (10%) | 12 |

## Summary

- **Detection rate (standard attacks): 251/270 (93.0%)**
- **Alert precision: 84.8%** (290 alerts on attacks, 52 on normal activity)
- **False alarms: 8.7 per 1,000 user-days** (about 0.35 per day for a 40-person team)

## Where the false alarms come from

| Rule | False alarms (all runs) | Typical cause in the simulation |
|---|---|---|
| `new_country` | 28 | a colleague genuinely travelling |
| `download_burst` | 19 | a heavy day for a normally quiet user |
| `off_hours` | 4 | an unusually early or late but legitimate login |
| `impossible_travel` | 1 | first login abroad shortly after one at home |

## Known blind spots

The *evasive* rows are attacks designed to stay under the thresholds: a password guesser that waits 5 minutes between tries, and a data thief who downloads at twice their normal pace spread over a full day. Fixed per-window thresholds miss these by design. Catching them would need longer-window baselines (for example daily totals per user) or per-IP reputation, at the cost of more false alarms.

`off_hours` is deliberately cautious: it stays silent until a person has at least 30 logins in the previous 30 days, and once someone has signed in at a given hour, later logins at that hour no longer look unusual. The missed night logins in the simulation are these two cases (new-ish accounts, or a second night attack on the same person).

`new_country` alerts on genuine travel are expected: they are medium severity and meant to be confirmed with the person, not to block them.
